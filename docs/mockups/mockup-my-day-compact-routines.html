Design recommendation

The section should preserve awareness without competing with the task list.

Use this adaptive hybrid as the default. It combines the summary bar’s progress signal with the grid’s scanability and direct actions.

Why it works

  • The donut counts only routines promoted for today, so optional completions do not distort the day’s commitment.
  • Routine-defined icons sit inside each completion control, preserving compactness while making the target recognizable.
  • The grid automatically moves between two and three columns based on available width.
  • Due or overdue flexible routines join the promoted list; on-track routines stay in a lower “Other routines” disclosure.

Overflow and completion

  • Show at most six routines initially, then reveal the remainder inline. Avoid numbered pages: pages hide checklist state and make items move unpredictably.
  • When every promoted routine is complete, collapse automatically to a compact “All routines complete” summary; the user can immediately reopen it.
  • Completed routines appear last in the full expanded grid with a muted checked treatment.

Ranking proposal: overdue flexible → due today flexible → scheduled daily/specific-day → other daily → on-track flexible in “Other routines.”

Use only if “do the next routine” is the product goal. It is compact and focused, but hides the remaining commitments.

Strengths

  • Very low vertical footprint and strong single-action focus.
  • Works naturally on narrow screens and touch devices.
  • Can automatically advance after completion.

Risks

  • Users must rotate to understand the day’s full routine load.
  • Previous/next controls add interaction cost for only three items.
  • Auto-rotation should be avoided; it creates motion and control problems.

A good optional “focus mode,” not the best default overview.

Strengths

  • Only 52px tall in its resting state.
  • Progress, remaining count, and routine names are still visible.
  • Quick-check buttons preserve one-click completion on desktop.

Risks

  • Icon-only quick checks need tooltips and accessible names.
  • Long routine names truncate quickly.
  • Less effective if routines are a primary daily workflow.
